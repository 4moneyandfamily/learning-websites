LESSON 00

First decide what the workspace is for.

Notion rewards a clear job. "Organize my life" is not a job. "Track every staffing agency, application, and interview until I am hired" is a job.

Strong fit

  • A job hunt with 11 agencies, dozens of applications, and interviews to prep.
  • A trading journal with a fixed daily structure and review cadence.
  • School deadlines across courses that need one calendar.
  • A dispute ledger where every claim needs dates, amounts, and documents.

Weak fit

  • Live balances or prices. Notion is not a dashboard for changing numbers.
  • Anything that must run while you sleep. Pair it with an automation tool instead.
  • Secrets, credentials, or account numbers. It is a workspace, not a vault.
  • A second brain with no capture habit. The tool cannot want it for you.
→

Your course project

Build a Command Center: one page with linked views of Agencies, Applications, Interviews, Assignments, and Trades. Every later lesson adds one layer to it. By Lesson 08 it runs your week.

LESSON 01

Everything is a block.

This is the whole mental model, and it is simpler than it sounds. A page is a stack of blocks. A database is a page whose children follow a schema. A view is just a lens on the same data.

1Block

The atom. Text, heading, image, embed, or a database. Press / to summon any of them.

→
2Page

A stack of blocks with a title. Pages nest inside pages, forever.

→
3Database

A page with a schema. Every row is itself a full page.

→
4View

Table, Board, Calendar, Timeline, Gallery. Same rows, different lens.

BLOCK

The atom

Every paragraph, heading, image, and database on a page is a block. Drag them, nest them, turn one type into another.

PAGE

The container

A titled stack of blocks. Open any database row and you get a page with room for notes, files, and sub-pages.

DATABASE

Structure plus pages

A spreadsheet where every row opens into a document. Properties are the columns; the page body holds the rest.

PROPERTY

One typed column

Select, Date, Relation, Formula. If you will ever filter or sort by it, give it a real type, never plain text.

VIEW

A saved lens

Same database, shown as a board grouped by status or a calendar of dates. Views do not copy data.

RELATION

The connective tissue

A property that links rows across databases. Applications point at Agencies; both sides can see the link.

→

The CS50 translation

You already know arrays of structs. A database is an array of structs where each struct opens into a document. A relation is a pointer. A rollup is a reduce function. Nothing here is new, only the costume.

LESSON 04

Properties are types. Views are lenses.

Two ideas, and they compose into everything advanced. Pick the property type that matches how you will query the data, then look at the same rows five different ways.

Reach for a real type when

  • You will filter, sort, or group by the value. Status, Select, Date, Person.
  • You will compute from it. Number and Date feed formulas and rollups.
  • You will link it. Relation is the only type that connects databases.
  • You want guardrails. Select beats free text for anything with a fixed set.

Leave it as text when

  • The value is truly free-form: names, one-line notes, URLs you only click.
  • No view will ever group or filter by it.
  • No formula will ever read it.
  • You are tempted to encode structure with commas. That is a relation instead.
TABLE

The default grid

Every property is a column. Best for data entry and for seeing everything at once.

BOARD

Kanban by status

Group the Agencies database by Status and drag cards between columns. Same rows, new shape.

CALENDAR

Time as the axis

Point it at Next step. Monday shows AppleOne at 10:00 AM and Express at 11:00 AM without any extra work.

TIMELINE

Ranges, not points

For date ranges like a lease decision window or a promo countdown. Bars instead of dots.

GALLERY

Cards with covers

Nice for visual collections. Overkill for agencies; right for a property or inspiration board.

LIST

Minimal rows

Titles only, fast to scan. Good for a reading list or a simple queue.

Notion · formula example
Days until the next step, as a Formula property:
dateBetween(prop("Next step"), now(), "days")
Negative means overdue. Pair it with a filter: Days left < 3.
!

Views never duplicate data

Deleting a view deletes the lens, not the rows. The one exception is deleting the original full-page view of a database, which deletes the database. Linked views on other pages are always safe to remove.

Connect databases and they start thinking.

One database is a list. Two databases with a relation is a system. This lesson wires Applications to Agencies and Interviews to Applications, then lets rollups do the counting.

1Agencies

The eleven you built. The authority list.

→
2Applications

Each row points at one Agency via Relation.

→
3Interviews

Each row points at one Application.

→
4Command Center

Linked views of all three, filtered per section.

01

Create the Applications database

Properties: Company (Title), Role (Text), Agency (Relation to Agencies, two-way on), Status (Select: Applied, Screening, Interview, Offer, Rejected), Applied (Date), Pay (Text). Add two real rows: the AppleOne and Express opportunities.

02

Add a Rollup on Agencies

On the Agencies database, add a Rollup property. Relation: Applications. Property: Company. Calculate: Count. You now see, per agency, how many applications it produced, updating itself forever.

03

Create the Interviews database

Properties: Interview (Title), Application (Relation, two-way), When (Date with time), Type (Select: Phone, Virtual, On-site), Prep (Text). Add Monday: AppleOne virtual 10:00 AM, Express on-site 11:00 AM.

04

Build the Command Center dashboard

On the Command Center page, type /linked and add a linked view of Interviews as a Calendar filtered to the next 14 days, plus a linked view of Agencies as a Board grouped by Status. The databases live once; the dashboard just looks at them.

→

The pattern to reuse

Trades point at Strategies. Assignments point at Courses. Disputes point at Agencies or furnishers. One authority database, many children, rollups counting upward. Learn it once on the job hunt and it ports everywhere.

!

Rollups have a ceiling

A rollup cannot read another rollup, and each rollup does exactly one calculation. If a question needs chained math across two relations, put an intermediate Formula on the middle database and roll that up instead.

04 · Properties & views  ·  05 · Relations & rollups  ·  06 · Templates & automations
LESSON 06

Stop repeating yourself on purpose.

Templates stamp out structured pages. Buttons run multi-step edits in one click. Automations react to changes. In that order of power, and of risk.

01

Add an Interview Prep template

In the Interviews database, open the dropdown next to New and create a template. Pre-fill the page body: company research, three questions to ask, what to bring, follow-up draft. Every Monday interview now starts from the same professional baseline.

02

Add a button that logs a touch

On an Agency page, insert a Button block that adds a timestamped note to the page. One click after every recruiter call, and the history writes itself. Buttons can add pages, edit properties, and insert blocks.

03

Automate the boring handoff

Database automations trigger on events: when an Application's Status becomes Interview, create an Interviews row pre-linked to it. Test it on one fake row first. Automations that create records are safe; automations that notify people get tested twice.

Automate when

  • The trigger and the action are both boring and well defined.
  • A missed step has a visible, reversible cost.
  • You have watched it run correctly three times by hand.
  • The worst failure mode is a duplicate you can delete.

Keep manual when

  • It sends anything to another human.
  • It deletes or archives records.
  • You cannot describe the trigger in one sentence.
  • It touches money, applications, or legal deadlines.

LESSON 08

Share like it is already public.

Notion makes sharing one click easy, which is exactly why this lesson exists. Set the habit now while the workspace is solo.

Who can see a page I share?

Whoever holds the link, at the permission you set: view, comment, or edit. Public links are indexed by search engines unless you disable it. Default to the narrowest access and widen deliberately.

What should never live in Notion?

Passwords, API keys, full account or card numbers, and anything you would not want in a breach disclosure. Notion is not a vault and not encrypted for secrets. Last-four masks are fine; full numbers are not.

How do I back it up?

Export pages or whole workspaces from Settings as Markdown or HTML, on a schedule you will actually keep. An export is a snapshot, not a sync; set a monthly reminder.

Can I undo a bad change?

Page history covers 7 days on Free, 30 on Plus, 90 on Business. Beyond that window, only your exports save you. Another reason the monthly export matters.

Guests versus members?

Guests are free-ish collaborators on specific pages; members bill per seat. Share a single filtered view with a guest before ever adding a member.

What about the AI reading my data?

AI features process your content on Notion's infrastructure. Keep genuinely sensitive material, legal strategy, account specifics, out of AI-processed pages if that bothers you. Enterprise buys zero data retention; you are not on Enterprise.
